# Stakeholder/Meeting Variant Recipes

## V-A — Stakeholder analysis

**When this variant:** Project kickoff, before launching change initiative, when scoping who to engage and how.

**Audience:** Project lead / PM, BA themselves (working artifact), sometimes shared with sponsor.

**Variant-specific questions:**
1. What's the initiative / project scope?
2. Is the team already known, or are we identifying who should be involved?
3. Any politically sensitive context to be aware of? (Existing tensions, ongoing reorgs)
4. How public is this analysis? (BA working note vs shared with stakeholders themselves)

**Variant-specific pitfalls:**
- Identifying only obvious roles (product, dev) — missing Compliance, Legal, Audit, Security, downstream teams
- Assigning power/interest based on assumption, not observation
- Writing "engagement strategy" as platitudes ("communicate regularly") — be specific
- Naming individuals in a deliverable that's shared broadly

**Blind spots:**
- "Shadow" stakeholders — informal advisors, executive sponsors' bosses, key customers' influence on internal teams
- Time-shifted stakeholders — someone who'll matter at go-live but isn't involved now (Operations, Customer Support)
- Disrupted parties whose work changes (not just who benefits)

## V-B — RACI matrix

**When this variant:** Project planning phase, ambiguity about who decides / does what, conflict resolution.

**Audience:** Whole project team, sponsor, sometimes wider org.

**Variant-specific questions:**
1. Is this RACI for a specific phase (planning, dev, launch) or whole project?
2. Are roles already defined (you have a team chart), or being defined as part of this exercise?
3. Are there external roles (vendor, partner) that need RACI placement?

**Variant-specific pitfalls:**
- "RA" on same person without explicit consideration — sometimes right (working manager) but often a sign of too-small team or unclear authority
- Listing too many roles per activity — every "C" must add value, not be courtesy
- Granularity mismatch — some activities at task level, others at phase level
- Skipping decision rights vs execution rights distinction

**Blind spots:**
- Decisions about change to scope, schedule, or budget (often missed; usually need senior accountability)
- Incident response / escalation paths
- Communication decisions (who decides what's said externally)

## V-C — Meeting minutes

**When this variant:** After a meeting (planning, decision-making, review, retrospective).

**Audience:** Attendees first, then wider distribution per meeting type.

**Variant-specific questions:**
1. What was the meeting about (purpose)?
2. Distribution list — attendees only, or broader?
3. Action item owners — already known, or being assigned in the meeting?
4. Is this a recurring meeting (so previous minutes provide context)?

**Variant-specific pitfalls:**
- Verbatim notes instead of synthesis (minutes vs transcript)
- Mixing decisions with discussions — they should be visually distinct
- Action items without owner / deadline
- Capturing political subtext that should stay verbal
- Inconsistent format across meetings (makes recurring meeting series hard to track)

**Blind spots:**
- Dissent — when someone disagreed but the decision proceeded, capture the dissent without making it personal
- Implicit decisions — "we'll figure it out next week" is an implicit decision to defer; capture as parking lot
- Off-meeting context — was there a side conversation that affected this? (Sometimes worth noting, sometimes not)

## V-D — Communication plan

**When this variant:** Project planning, kickoff, or when current communication is misfiring (over/under/wrong audience).

**Audience:** Project lead, BA themselves, sometimes communications/PR team.

**Variant-specific questions:**
1. What's the initiative / project the plan covers?
2. Stakeholder analysis already exists (V-A), or build inline?
3. Any existing comm cadence to integrate with (org-level standups, monthly reports)?
4. Anti-patterns to avoid (e.g., "Last project had too many status emails, want to streamline")?

**Variant-specific pitfalls:**
- One-size-fits-all frequency (same monthly email to executives, customers, and dev team — none of them want the same thing)
- Forgetting crisis / incident comm path (steady-state plan only)
- Channel mismatch — sending sensitive risk info via broad email
- No feedback loop — comm only one-way, no listening built in

**Blind spots:**
- External communication (customers, partners, regulators) — often handled separately
- Skip-level communication (sponsor's boss occasionally wants to hear directly)
- Crisis path — when something goes wrong, what's the cadence and who decides

---

## When variants compose

For a new project kickoff:
1. **V-A** first — who's involved, what they care about
2. **V-B** second — for each major activity, who R/A/C/I
3. **V-D** third — for each stakeholder identified in V-A, comm cadence

V-C (meeting minutes) is standalone but often references V-A's stakeholders. Use the same role names for consistency across documents.
